# Validated findings

Things we have measured or observed in prototypes, as opposed to decisions (docs/DECISIONS.md) or ideas (docs/IDEAS.md). Each entry names the experiment that supports it.

1. **Piece-level rearrangement is easy; class layout is the real constraint.** Without class maps, the solver reaches zero shared adjacencies in under a second at every size and geometry (experiments 001, 007). With class maps, residual overlap comes from large single-class regions (sky 21, meadow 24 of 80 in direction A), not from search: 7× more iterations and 5 seeds change nothing (007). Quarter rotation roughly halves the residual (002, 006, 007).
2. **Same-class pieces are interchangeable, so scene-level carry-over must be measured.** Direction A's three landscapes had 66–71% of cells with the same class at the same position; a blind evaluator called them "variations of one picture" (002). Scene-level metrics predicted this before rendering and are now gates (`metapuzzle scene-metrics`).
3. **Passing the class-level gates is necessary but not sufficient for perceived distinctiveness.** Direction B passed every gate (9–14% same-position) and still scored 2/5 on distinctiveness with no subject recognized (006). Evaluators key on palette and motif vocabulary, which is shared by construction; perceived difference must come from bold, nameable macro-silhouettes.
4. **Tone-map quantization produces genuinely different subjects from one histogram.** Rank-quantized cat / sailboat / tree at 20×25 scored 4/5 on distinctiveness with zero piece-level shared adjacencies (004). Tree was recognized, sailboat partly, cat not; equal counts per tone class scattered the light ground and the grid was fully visible (5/5).
5. **Any colour or texture step that lands on a piece edge is visible as a grid line; stochastic textures themselves join invisibly.** In every grid review (002 ×2, 006) all visible edges were class boundaries on cuts, never same-texture seams. The in-piece masked transitions hide the boundaries they cover (002 v2); boundaries without a transition class stay visible.
6. **Transition pieces cost histogram freedom.** With tone boundaries inside transition pieces, the number of transition pieces equals the perimeter of each tone region, which differs between subjects (cat vs sailboat: 126 pieces apart at 20×25) (005 step 1). Slack comes from letting interior regions absorb some transition pieces as texture variation, solved by the role-map tool.
7. **The border is the only hard geometric limit.** Each side needs at least 6 non-corner pieces for zero shared adjacency without rotation; the same border pieces frame all three images unless edge pieces rotate or flat-sided pieces may sit inside (005-piece-geometry, 001).
8. **80 pieces (8×10) is too coarse to carry a nameable subject unless it is a single bold silhouette.** Direction A's tree was recognized; its island, sail, summit and hut (3–4 cells) were not (002). Direction B's sunflower, bay and pond were not (006).
9. **The hybrid tone-field grammar passes the piece-level gate at 500 pieces with readable subjects (experiment 005).** Cat / sailboat / tree at 20×25 with every tier boundary inside a transition piece: shared adjacencies 0/0/0, distinctiveness 4/5, grid verdicts 2–3/5 (best so far; about 90% of grid lines invisible in the tree image). The tree is recognized; the cat and boat silhouettes are not yet (small head, sail light-on-light). Transition lobes that are round and cell-sized read as a "lattice of holes" and betray the grid rhythm.
10. **Scene-level carry-over numbers are inherently high for tone-field pictures** (same-position 43–46%, 2×2 reuse 71–75%) because half the pieces are background. With distinct conventional cuts this cannot be exploited physically (see docs/DECISIONS.md); it remains a difficulty indicator.
11. **Current solutions make connectors effectively interchangeable (experiment 010).** Because each piece side meets a different partner in each solution, sides fall into "mating classes" that must share one shape. In every solution so far the largest class holds 46–91% of all sides, so a physical cut would allow hundreds of unintended fits per intended one (false-fit ratio 60–530). The minimum is a class of 3 tabs + 3 blanks (asymmetric connectors) or 4 sides (symmetric connectors, where the three solutions are the three perfect matchings of K4), both with zero false fits. Reaching that needs a solver objective that closes partner sets into minimal blocks; it is a strong extra constraint but would make the three solutions mechanically encoded with bounded trial-and-error.
12. **Boundary length is part of the shared inventory (experiment 005, step 5).** With every tier boundary inside a transition piece, each picture needs as many transition pieces per tone pair as it has boundary edges of that pair (plus corners). The sailboat's hull-on-sea boundary needed 79 dark/mid straights where the cat needed 31, so a per-class maximum exceeded the dark tier total and no common histogram existed. Consequences: transition classes must be merged by rotation symmetry (the same physical piece serves both sides of a boundary), and the three subjects must be designed to matching "boundary budgets" per tone pair, the coupled-design constraint the handoff anticipated (section 8). Small residues can be absorbed as texture variation next to boundaries.
13. **The owner's three steers are now design constraints, not open options (docs/DECISIONS.md, 2026-10-27).** (1) Lines first: shape and piece mapping are explored as black lines on white (contours only) before fills, textures and details, and every scene is evaluated in that ink mode before its full render. (2) Each image must have a unique solution with few wrong local ones, so pieces must be distinct both mechanically and visually: connector mating classes should sit at the minimum (K(3,3) for asymmetric connectors, K4 for symmetric or andro, with false-fit ratio 0 as the target, checked with `metapuzzle connectors`, T8) and distinguishing marks should separate pieces within each tone region (checked by the sampled interchangeability test, T9). (3) Shared adjacency need only be "not practically reusable": the piece-level gate is relaxed to below 10% per pair with no transferable cluster larger than 3 pieces, zero remaining a stretch, and the solver may trade a few shared adjacencies for distinct connectors. The mechanical constraint is still unmet: in experiment 010 the largest class holds 46–91% of sides in every solution set so far, so the connector target is the open work item.
14. **Ink mode is the right diagnostic (experiment 005, run-ink-v3).** Rendering only the transition boundaries as black strokes on white shows the structure the textures hide: the side-biased transition classes (v1–v3) produce contour segments that do not meet at cuts, and surplus transitions placed next to real boundaries produce doubled contours along the trunk and hull. The marching-tile scheme (mid-edge-pinned paths) fixes the first; the second means surplus must be minimized by matching boundary budgets in the subject design, with any residue placed as isolated marks far from boundaries (or used deliberately as small motifs), never adjacent to a boundary.
