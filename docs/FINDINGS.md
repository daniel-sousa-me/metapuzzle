# Validated findings

Things we have measured or observed in prototypes, as opposed to decisions (docs/DECISIONS.md) or ideas (docs/IDEAS.md). Each entry names the experiment that supports it.

1. **Piece-level rearrangement is easy; class layout is the real constraint.** Without class maps, the solver reaches zero shared adjacencies in under a second at every size and geometry (experiments 001, 007). With class maps, residual overlap comes from large single-class regions (sky 21, meadow 24 of 80 in direction A), not from search: 7× more iterations and 5 seeds change nothing (007). Quarter rotation roughly halves the residual (002, 006, 007).
2. **Same-class pieces are interchangeable, so scene-level carry-over must be measured.** Direction A's three landscapes had 66–71% of cells with the same class at the same position; a blind evaluator called them "variations of one picture" (002). Scene-level metrics predicted this before rendering and are now gates (`metapuzzle scene-metrics`).
3. **Passing the class-level gates is necessary but not sufficient for perceived distinctiveness.** Direction B passed every gate (9–14% same-position) and still scored 2/5 on distinctiveness with no subject recognized (006). Evaluators key on palette and motif vocabulary, which is shared by construction; perceived difference must come from bold, nameable macro-silhouettes.
4. **Tone-map quantization produces genuinely different subjects from one histogram.** Rank-quantized cat / sailboat / tree at 20×25 scored 4/5 on distinctiveness with zero piece-level shared adjacencies (004). Tree was recognized, sailboat partly, cat not; equal counts per tone class scattered the light ground and the grid was fully visible (5/5).
5. **Any colour or texture step that lands on a piece edge is visible as a grid line; stochastic textures themselves join invisibly.** In every grid review (002 ×2, 006) all visible edges were class boundaries on cuts, never same-texture seams. The in-piece masked transitions hide the boundaries they cover (002 v2); boundaries without a transition class stay visible.
6. **Transition pieces cost histogram freedom.** With tone boundaries inside transition pieces, the number of transition pieces equals the perimeter of each tone region, which differs between subjects (cat vs sailboat: 126 pieces apart at 20×25) (005 step 1). Slack comes from letting interior regions absorb some transition pieces as texture variation, solved by the role-map tool.
7. **The border is the only hard geometric limit.** Each side needs at least 6 non-corner pieces for zero shared adjacency without rotation; the same border pieces frame all three images unless edge pieces rotate or flat-sided pieces may sit inside (005-piece-geometry, 001).
8. **80 pieces (8×10) is too coarse to carry a nameable subject unless it is a single bold silhouette.** Direction A's tree was recognized; its island, sail, summit and hut (3–4 cells) were not (002). Direction B's sunflower, bay and pond were not (006).
9. **The hybrid tone-field grammar passes the piece-level gate at 500 pieces with readable subjects (experiment 005).** Cat / sailboat / tree at 20×25 with every tier boundary inside a transition piece: shared adjacencies 0/0/0, distinctiveness 4/5, grid verdicts 2–3/5 (best so far; about 90% of grid lines invisible in the tree image). The tree is recognized; the cat and boat silhouettes are not yet (small head, sail light-on-light). Transition lobes that are round and cell-sized read as a "lattice of holes" and betray the grid rhythm.
10. **Scene-level carry-over numbers are inherently high for tone-field pictures** (same-position 43–46%, 2×2 reuse 71–75%) because half the pieces are background. With distinct conventional cuts this cannot be exploited physically (see docs/DECISIONS.md); it remains a difficulty indicator.
11. **Current solutions make connectors effectively interchangeable (experiment 010).** Because each piece side meets a different partner in each solution, sides fall into "mating classes" that must share one shape. In every solution so far the largest class holds 46–91% of all sides, so a physical cut would allow hundreds of unintended fits per intended one (false-fit ratio 60–530). The minimum is a class of 3 tabs + 3 blanks (asymmetric connectors) or 4 sides (symmetric connectors, where the three solutions are the three perfect matchings of K4), both with zero false fits. Reaching that needs a solver objective that closes partner sets into minimal blocks; it is a strong extra constraint but would make the three solutions mechanically encoded with bounded trial-and-error.
12. **Boundary length is part of the shared inventory (experiment 005, step 5).** With every tier boundary inside a transition piece, each picture needs as many transition pieces per tone pair as it has boundary edges of that pair (plus corners). The sailboat's hull-on-sea boundary needed 79 dark/mid straights where the cat needed 31, so a per-class maximum exceeded the dark tier total and no common histogram existed. Consequences: transition classes must be merged by rotation symmetry (the same physical piece serves both sides of a boundary), and the three subjects must be designed to matching "boundary budgets" per tone pair, the coupled-design constraint the handoff anticipated (section 8). Small residues can be absorbed as texture variation next to boundaries.
13. **The owner's three steers are now design constraints, not open options (docs/DECISIONS.md, 2026-10-27).** (1) Lines first: shape and piece mapping are explored as black lines on white (contours only) before fills, textures and details, and every scene is evaluated in that ink mode before its full render. (2) Each image must have a unique solution with few wrong local ones, so pieces must be distinct both mechanically and visually: connector mating classes should sit at the minimum (K(3,3) for asymmetric connectors, K4 for symmetric or andro, with false-fit ratio 0 as the target, checked with `metapuzzle connectors`, T8) and distinguishing marks should separate pieces within each tone region (checked by the sampled interchangeability test, T9). (3) Shared adjacency need only be "not practically reusable": the piece-level gate is relaxed to below 10% per pair with no transferable cluster larger than 3 pieces, zero remaining a stretch, and the solver may trade a few shared adjacencies for distinct connectors. The mechanical constraint is still unmet: in experiment 010 the largest class holds 46–91% of sides in every solution set so far, so the connector target is the open work item. (Note, 2026-10-27: DECISIONS entry "Mechanical uniqueness: rely on artwork" makes this target non-blocking; T8 is recorded, and T9 decides.)
14. **Ink mode is the right diagnostic (experiment 005, run-ink-v3).** Rendering only the transition boundaries as black strokes on white shows the structure the textures hide: the side-biased transition classes (v1–v3) produce contour segments that do not meet at cuts, and surplus transitions placed next to real boundaries produce doubled contours along the trunk and hull. The marching-tile scheme (mid-edge-pinned paths) fixes the first; the second means surplus must be minimized by matching boundary budgets in the subject design, with any residue placed as isolated marks far from boundaries (or used deliberately as small motifs), never adjacent to a boundary.
15. **A third of transition pieces were mis-rotated in every render before 2026-10-27.** Scene tokens at rotation 0 were treated as unconstrained, so in quarter-rotation mode the solver rotated 153 of 483 forced transition cells (experiment 005 v3) at will. Part of the notched, blocky look of runs v1–v3 came from this, not from the mask design. Fixed in scene.py (bare and @0 tokens of any class that carries an explicit rotation anywhere are now forced; `@*` is the free escape). All earlier transition renders should be re-read with this in mind.
16. **Annealing cannot make connectors unique; construction can (experiment 010, round 2).** A mating-class penalty in the annealer halves false fits on tiny boards but barely moves them at 10×8 or 25×20 (largest class stays above 95% of sides): one leaked contact merges blocks, and the frame and class maps constrain the rest. A constructive design (group translations giving a 1-factorization) achieves exact K(3,3) blocks, zero shared adjacencies and a false-fit ratio of about 0.1 on tray boards whose cell count is divisible by 3, but it is not yet compatible with standard borders, K4 (rotation) or class maps. Mechanical uniqueness therefore needs the three placements to be designed algebraically together with the pictures, not searched afterwards; until then, uniqueness must come mostly from visually distinct pieces (chip signatures, motifs) and conventional distinct cuts.
17. **With correct orientations and hosting, the tone-field grammar renders cleanly; surplus pieces are the last big defect (005 v5, 6b).** Blind test: tree 5/5, boat partly, cat not (silhouette resolution/pose); distinctiveness 4/5. Grid review: about 90% of grid-line length invisible, no same-texture seams; verdict still 4/5 because 25–40 surplus transition pieces per image appear as hard-edged foreign-colour blocks inside regions, read as mistakes. The sail's diagonal shows a cell-sized staircase (the new `diag` tile addresses it). Ink mode confirmed continuous contours. Next levers: match boundary budgets (by design and by automatic boundary nudging), use higher resolution (about 1000 pieces), and richer contour tiles.
18. **The olive halo around dark figures is a quantization artefact, not a recipe problem (005 inspection).** Anti-aliased tone targets give every cell on a dark/light silhouette edge a mid tone, so an M ring forms around the figure (84 D cells touch only M in 005 v2) and 2–2 ties in transition classification resolved toward the darker tier. Fix: paint targets as tier label maps and downsample by majority vote (mid tier only where deliberately painted), and break classification ties toward the lighter tier.
19. **Diagonal tiles only help at exactly 45° (005 v6).** The tree canopy's octagon edges became clean diagonals, but the sail's 2.5:1 slope has no 45° runs and stays a staircase; bumps and notches never applied. Generalizing would mean contours crossing piece edges at several standardized ports (quarter points), multiplying classes and surplus. Chosen direction instead: higher resolution (about 1000 pieces makes one-cell steps small) and subjects drawn with 0°, 45° and 90° edges where a clean line matters.
20. **Automatic budget equalization works but must flip the right cells (005 step 7).** Nudging at most 24 cells per image brought surplus from 41/33/44 to 0/1/0, and the full render lost all stray patches with the grid essentially invisible. But flips were restricted to D–M and M–L, so dark figures acquired olive bites. Erosion/dilation of a figure against the light background (D–L flips along the silhouette) is far less visible than a tone substitution inside it, and should be the preferred move; the number of flips also shrinks with resolution.
21. **Equalizer v2 (erode/dilate moves) keeps the figures and cuts surplus by three quarters (005 step 7b).** Surplus 41/33/44 → 10/7/10 with 16–22 flips per image, none inside figures; most sky specks gone, silhouettes intact. Remaining olive bites in the cat come from the anti-aliased v2 tiers (finding 18), not from the equalizer. The production pipeline is therefore: label-map targets → matched-budget subjects → equalizer v2 for the residue → marching tiles.
22. **Resolution pays off between 500 and 1000 pieces (experiment 013).** Same subjects at 500/750/1026/1536 pieces: zero shared adjacencies at every size; the cat's ears and tail and the boat's sail read from 750 up and are unambiguous at 1026; ink views show no grid rhythm at any size. (Correction, see entry 30: the 27x38 blind test did not name the placeholder cat; the "unambiguous" reading was a visual read by the author, not a blind test.) Surplus transition pieces scale with perimeter (38/20/34 → 75/51/81), so the equalizer or matched budgets are needed at every size. Compute is negligible (solve under 0.1 s, render about a minute at 1536).
23. **The value-inversion subject set delivers distinctiveness (experiment 011, first render).** "Field, Wood and Water" at 27×38 with matched boundary budgets: shared adjacencies 0/0/0, surplus 29/26/21 after equalization, distinctiveness 5/5 in ink and 4/5 in colour; the owl is recognized blind (read as an owl-or-cat face), the hare reads in ink but not in colour, the swan reads only partly. Remaining work is per-subject silhouette design (owl cues, swan neck and water, hare pose and figure/ground contrast), ragged transitions for the chip tier, drawing at 27×38 instead of resampling, and placing surplus inside figures as texture rather than at the margins.
24. **Native 27×38 drawing fixed the hare and swan; the owl needs species cues (011 v2).** Redrawn set: hare 4/5 and swan 3/5 recognized blind in colour, distinctiveness 4/5, grid 2/5 for two pictures; the owl reads as a face (4/5) but is called a skull, because eyes + rectangular beak + dark body is not owl-specific. Surplus pieces (about 20–25 per image) remain the visible blemish and their strokes dominate the ink view; class-level budget matching is the fix. Ink recognition lags colour for A and C (65% and 55% continuous contours), so the ink test is the stricter gate.
25. **Faint chip signatures do not make fields solvable (011 v4).** They collapse the scene-level overlap metrics (weighted overlap 0.5–1.2%) without changing the solvability score (2/5): about 70% of pieces remain visually interchangeable and the plain sky and meadow cover 50–60% of each picture. Since connector shapes cannot be made distinct (finding 16 and experiment 012), uniform fields give the solver no feedback at all. Background pieces therefore need legible self-contained motifs assigned by region, as the reference images do, so that sorting by motif narrows placement and the same motif re-reads across pictures.
26. **Field solvability on the first redrawn 27x38 set (T9 and grid review, `011-subjects/ambiguity-and-grid-v2.md`).** About 65 to 75% of the 1026 pieces look interchangeable with at least one other piece, mostly the plain L, M and D pieces that tile the sky, the meadow and the night field. The distinct pieces are the transitions (families of a few members), the motif and dot pieces, and the S and W chips. The hardest regions are the pale sky and the olive body interior, 60 to 70% of the frame. Swapping interior same-class pieces is undetectable; swapping boundary pieces of one class gives a one-cell jog; a misplaced surplus insert leaves an obvious gap. Solvability scored 2/5. The grid review of the owl and swan found 85 to 90% of interior grid lines out of sight, with verdicts B 3/5 and C 4/5; the strongest tells are isolated single-piece foreign-colour patches (about 20 in B, 30 in C) and the rectangular nose inset.
27. **Class-level budget matching cut the surplus but missed its target (011 pipeline v3: `make_targets_v3.py`, `budget_v3.py`, `27x38-v3/budgets.txt`).** Class surplus fell from 193 to 107 (51/26/30) when the three pictures' transition classes were matched cell by cell; the target of under 30 was not met. Frame edge surplus fell from 330 to 258 and corners stayed at 15, because the owl's frame has no dark cells and dark is not a background tier for it. Tier totals are not exact (D 285/287/286). Before that, equalization of the redrawn set had cut the surplus from 193 to 127 (50/36/41), and the roles stage then stopped because the hare needed 11 dark hosts and only 10 far dark cells existed (`experiments/011-subjects/README.md`).
28. **Piece-level results of the redrawn 27x38 set (011 `27x38-v2`).** `run-full` and `run-ink` each share one contact (one BC pair, a cluster of two; 0/0/1), which passes the piece gate. `run-full-v4` (chip sub-classes) and `run-ink-v4` each share one contact in AC. Scene gates still fail for `run-full`: 2x2 window reuse 71.3, 64.3 and 68.3% (limit 30%), and transferable blob 10.3 and 11.5% of A (limit 10%).
29. **A standard border is not reachable with the current seams (011 `border-check`).** At 27x38 with the standard border, quarter rotation and the v9 scenes, a run with no extra flat pieces fails before solving (border class counts differ between A and B on the edge). With 20, 40 or 80 extra flat pieces, all three runs are invalid, each with 10 mismatched contacts. Seams pair only pieces of the same class, and each class's leftover flat edges differ in parity across the boards (A-B: D, L, S, W; A-C: M). Extra pieces add the same count to every board, so the parity stays. A standard frame needs matched border class counts or seams across classes; the tray remains the product form.
30. **Blind test at 27x38 with placeholder subjects (013 `blind-test-g27x38.md`).** The placeholder cat was not named at 27x38 (a pose problem, not resolution). The boat scored 5/5 blind for the first time, and the tree 3/5 (its octagonal canopy comes from 45-degree tiles). Distinctiveness 4/5; grid 3, 2 and 2 out of 5 for A, B and C. About 40 foreign blocks remained in A (unequalized surplus). This corrects entry 22: its "unambiguous" cat was the author's own visual read (the 013 README says so), not a blind test.
31. **Blind results for the v5 hybrid runs, 20x25 (005 `blind-test-ink-1.md`, `blind-test-3.md`, `grid-visibility-2.md`).** Ink, run-ink-v5: the boat was recognized for the first time (3/5; mast 4/5, hull 3/5), the tree 4/5, and the cat was not named (its ears read as towers); contours were about 70, 90 and 60% continuous for A, B and C; distinctiveness 4/5. Equalizer v2 (run-full-v5eq2): stray blocks fell to 2 to 5 per image from 25 to 40; the tree scored 4/5, the boat was read as a lighthouse (2/5), the cat was not named; distinctiveness 4/5 (A vs B 3/5); grid 4, 4 and 3. Grid review of run-full-v5: about 90% of grid-line length invisible in C, with verdicts C 4/5 and B 4/5, driven by about 40 and 25 foreign-colour patches (surplus).
32. **The automatic grid metric does not order the runs as humans do (009).** Human means: 004 5.0, 006 4.0, 002 v2 3.5, 002 v1 3.0. Metric mean visible fraction: 002 v1 0.636, 006 0.608, 002 v2 0.606, 004 0.433, so 004 is ranked last. No visibility ratio from 1.0 to 3.0 puts 004 first. The metric is not used as a gate. A whole-edge statistic with a 5 dE floor puts 002 last but still does not reproduce the order; it is a lead fitted to four runs only.
33. **Mixed frames became valid only with seam-aware construction (008, round 2).** Round 1 reported 0/0/0 shared adjacency on invalid solutions: 3 mismatched contacts with quarter rotation and 41 with 64 extra flat pieces, because the energy counted only shared adjacencies. Round 2 adds seam-aware placement, and both runs are valid. With quarter rotation, 127 flat-sided pieces give 15 to 23 seams per board and 0/0/0 shared. Without rotation, 237 to 245 flat-sided pieces give 70 to 82 seams, and 137 to 161 of the 380 to 396 interior pieces are flat-sided; shared adjacency is 0/2/2 without extras and 2/0/0 with 64 extras. Scene gates fail (2x2 window reuse 60.6 to 73.6%).
34. **Scene-strategy trials (003, metrics only, `fill.py`).** Three intent files were filled into class maps with one histogram, and only the scene metrics were run. Inversion passes the scene gates (2x2 window reuse, plain, 0.10 to 0.17). Skyline fails the same-position gate (27.5 to 30.0%, limit 25%). Frames fail the 2x2 window-reuse gate (33.8 to 42.9%, limit 30%). Design note 07 recommends inversion, which became the value-inversion subject set of experiment 011.
35. **Direction A blind tests and grid reviews (002).** Blind test 1 (run-none): only the tree-like A was read (canopy 5/5); B and C were not recognized; distinctiveness 3/5; tile-grid scores A 1, B 4, C 3. Blind test 2 (run-quarter, after the texture fixes): the tree was named 2/5, B and C were unnamed, distinctiveness fell to 2/5, and tile-grid scores were 3, 4, 3. Grid reviews: 7 of 16 interior lines (44%) invisible in run-none and in run-none-v2, with verdicts A 3/5 and C 4/5 (run-none) and A 3/5 and B 4/5 (run-none-v2). Quarter rotation cut shared adjacency to 2.8 to 3.5%, but the recognition failures did not change.
36. **The first motif layer overshoots (011 pipeline v5).** With 31% of pieces carrying motifs on near-regular spacing, scene gates pass for the first time and motif sorting gives about 40% of pieces a rough home, but blind recognition halves (owl 2/5, swan 2/5) because the symbols compete with the silhouettes, the ink view is overwhelmed, and lattice-like spacing reveals the grid. Target for v6: about 15–20% coverage in organic clusters with per-piece variation, a one-cell motif-free halo around every silhouette, and a light motif layer in ink.
37. **Recognition is limited by figure detail and scene cues, not by the motif layer (011 v6).** At 14% motif coverage with halo and variation the silhouettes dominate again, yet blind readers call the pictures "top-down maps" (olive landmass, face/skull, winding river): the pixel-art silhouettes lack internal lines (eye, ear, wing, tail), scene cues (horizon, water, moon) are weak, and surplus rectangles add a patchwork feel. The owl, which has internal features, is the only subject read consistently. Next: internal lines and scene cues in the design pass, surplus elimination, then re-test; motif density is a secondary dial.
38. **A figure-distance motif gradient is not computable from the v2 tiers (011 pipeline v7, `motifs/make_motif_maps_v3.py`).** Three attempts to place motifs by distance from the animal reached only 2 to 4% coverage against a 20% target, because the tiers carry no animal mask: in the hare picture the figure tier (M) is one 396-cell connected region, the hare plus its olive landmass, so a 2-cell halo around it covers most of the cream field; identical per-class counts then bind every picture to the smallest pool. In the dark-ground pictures the same rule misfires the other way unless figure and ground are defined per picture. Consequences: (a) the design pass must export a separate animal mask if figure-aware placement is wanted; (b) the fallback, the v6 carrier halo with density by distance from any non-plain cell (10/20/30% at 1/2/3+ cells), reached only 5.2% coverage because the cells next to a non-plain cell are all inside the halo and few plain cells lie 3 or more cells from every S, W or transition cell; the fields read as nearly bare (shared 2/1/0, scene gate B-C 31.3%). v7 is retired and v6 remains the motif baseline.
39. **The v4 draft fixes the owl and breaks the hare (011 `blind-test-v4-draft.md`, previews before the pipeline).** On the tone preview the owl is read as an owl-like face 4/5 (eyes with pupils are the carrier; the moon disc above reads as a hood), the hare 2/5 (the hedge band with a crown skyline reads as a tree canopy or hillside, and the hare silhouette is lost in the olive mass; it scored 4/5 in v2 without the hedge) and the swan 2/5 (a cream mass with a dashed olive band reads as a landscape; the neck is not read; the stippled water rectangle reads as a sign). On contours alone all three are 2/5. Lesson: a scene cue must not form a second large mass of the figure's tone next to the figure; internal lines help only once the silhouette reads.
40. **Class-level matching stalls near 80 surplus pieces with local moves (011 `make_targets_v4.py`, `27x38-v4/`, `27x38-v4-blocks/`).** Allowing figure-edge nudges cut the class surplus from 193 to 78 (A 42, B 22, C 14; by pair DM 10, DW 9, DL 16, SL 3, MW 10, ML 10, WL 20), against a target under 30. About 85% of single-cell candidates are rejected because they create tips, 1-wide strips or isolated cells, and domino or 2x2 block moves do not help (83). Tier totals drift by a few cells (W 36/30/36) once the tier weight is relaxed. Reaching under 30 needs a different approach: matching at design time (draw transitions to a shared budget, as the v4 draft does with spread 6 on edge and corner pools) rather than post-hoc cell moves, or accepting about 80 surplus pieces placed by the per-pair policy.
