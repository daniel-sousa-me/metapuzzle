# Blind recognition test — ink mode, v5 (run-ink-v5, 20x25)

Evaluator: fresh agent, shown only the three ink PNGs (black contour lines on white). Intended: A cat, B sailboat, C tree.

- **A**: "a tall two-towered building or pair of pillars with arched tops above a rippling water line." Cat not named (the ears read as arched towers; the body's internal surplus strokes read as ladder rungs). Contour about 70% continuous.
- **B**: "a sailboat seen side-on, with a mast, a triangular sail and a curved hull, over waves." Sailboat 3/5, mast 4/5, hull 3/5. Recognized. Contour about 90% continuous; mast and sail edges doubled.
- **C**: "a tree with a round canopy on a trunk, above a ground line." Tree 4/5. Recognized. Contour about 60% continuous (crown interior fragmented by surplus strokes).
- **Comparison**: three different subjects sharing one stroke language. Distinctiveness 4/5.
- **Scattered strokes**: read as deliberate rain/wind texture in A and B; closer to noise in C.

## Conclusions

1. Lines-first works as a design check: the boat is recognized for the first time, and the tree again; the cat silhouette needs a more cat-like pose (ears read as towers, no tail/head separation at this resolution).
2. Doubled contours and interior strokes come from surplus transition pieces placed next to boundaries; they are the main remaining defect and shrink as boundary budgets are matched in the subject design.
3. Surplus placed in open regions is tolerable when sparse and consistently oriented (reads as weather); random orientation inside a figure reads as noise.
