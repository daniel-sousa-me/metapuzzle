# Evaluations — pipeline v5 (motif layer) on 27x38-v2 tiers

## Blind, full render
- A hare: "a whimsical night landscape; a tall olive tree or upright figure" (3/5). Stars, leaves, ring-flowers and snowflakes named (3–4/5). Hare not named.
- B owl: "a navy, star-speckled body with two cream lobes" (2/5): eyes read as moons; owl lost.
- C swan: "a sprawling cream organic shape, perhaps an animal or cloud" (2/5).
- Distinctiveness 4/5; grid 3/3/4; about 15–30 single-cell foreign rectangles per picture (surplus) read as mistakes. B reads richer with motifs; A and C busier.

## Blind, ink render
- All three down to 1–3/5; "the small symbols mostly hurt reading: they fill the field at the same density as the contour." Contours 60–75% continuous.

## T9 solvability
- 55–65% of pieces still interchangeable (plain L/M/D). Motifs sort at a glance into 7–8 groups and cluster by region, so about 40% of pieces get a rough home. Score 2/5. Busy zones: cream top band, navy edges.

## Grid review
- 85–90% of grid lines invisible; the surplus rectangles are the main tell; motifs on near-regular spacing (dot triplets, ring rows) read as a lattice and reveal the grid.

## Conclusions
1. The motif layer overshoots: at 31% coverage with uniform spacing it competes with the silhouettes and halves recognition. Target for v6: about 15–20% coverage, organic clustering (drifts, constellations) with per-piece variation in size, offset and rotation, no motifs within one cell of a figure contour, and a lighter or optional motif layer in ink mode.
2. Motif pieces become individually placeable only if each is visually distinct AND the box picture shows them faithfully (it does); plain pieces remain the hard part, as skies are in ordinary puzzles.
3. Surplus elimination (matcher v4) remains the top visual fix; motif lattices must be jittered.
