# Blind recognition test 1 — "Field, Wood and Water", 27x38 ink render (run-ink)

Evaluator: fresh agent, shown only the three ink PNGs. Intended: A hare, B owl, C swan.

- **A**: "a seated rabbit-like figure with two long upright ears." Ears 4/5, body 3/5. Recognized (in ink the hare reads better than in colour, because the interior S strokes read as fur). Contour about 80% continuous.
- **B**: "a cat's face, front view, with pointed ears." Face 4/5, eyes 4/5. Owl read as cat (the designer's predicted risk: tufts and eyes). Contour about 85%.
- **C**: "an abstract, bird-like or humped animal; hard to name." Bird 2/5. Not recognized. Contour about 70%.
- **Comparison**: "different drawings, not variations." Distinctiveness 5/5.
- Stray strokes read as fur texture inside the hare, as noise at the margins in B and C (surplus pieces placed far from boundaries, and frame cuts).

## Conclusions

1. Distinctiveness is solved: 5/5 in ink, 4/5 in colour.
2. Per subject: the hare's ears carry it in ink but not in colour (olive on cream with a dark shadow confuses the figure/ground); the owl needs an owl-specific cue (round eyes with a beak, a branch, the moon disc behind it) to stop reading as a cat; the swan needs a stronger S-curve neck and head with a beak, and visible water.
3. Keep surplus strokes inside figures (fur, feathers, ripples) rather than at margins, where they read as noise.
