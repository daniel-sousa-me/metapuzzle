# Visual-ambiguity (T9) and grid review — subject set v2 (27x38-v2/run-full)

## T9 visual ambiguity (uniqueness by appearance)
- About 65–75% of the 1026 pieces look interchangeable with at least one other piece: the plain L, M and D pieces that tile the sky, meadow and night field.
- Distinctive: transition pieces (boundary families of a few members each), motif/dot pieces, S and W chip pieces.
- Hardest regions by appearance: the pale sky and the olive body interior, 60–70% of the frame.
- Wrong local arrangements: swapping interior same-class pieces is undetectable; swapping same-class boundary pieces gives a one-cell jog (subtle); misplacing a surplus insert leaves an orphan gap (obvious).
- Solvability score 2/5. Recommended change: give plain pieces individually identifiable marks (the chip-signature sub-classes, pipeline v4, are exactly this).

## Grid review (B owl, C swan)
- About 85–90% of interior grid lines invisible in B; verdicts B 3/5, C 4/5.
- The strongest grid tells are the isolated single-piece foreign-colour patches (about 20 in B, 30 in C) and the rectangular nose inset; the figure boundary is bubbly with one-cell notches whose rhythm partly reveals the grid.
- Recommended: eliminate isolated foreign-colour patches (surplus); give the beak a shape.

## Consequences
1. Solvability is now the dominant open problem: with 65–75% interchangeable pieces the puzzle would be trial and error in the fields. The chip sub-class layer (v4 pipeline) and distinct cuts are the planned remedy; T9 should be re-run on v4.
2. Surplus elimination (class-level matching) remains the top visual fix.
