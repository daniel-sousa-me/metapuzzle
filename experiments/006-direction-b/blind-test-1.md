# Blind recognition test 1 — direction B "Garden Terrazzo" (run-quarter, 8x10)

Evaluator: fresh agent, shown only the three solution PNGs. Intended: A The Sunflower (radial), B Sunset Bay (bands), C Koi Pond (top-down, diagonal path).

- **A**: "an abstract terrazzo-and-mosaic landscape: pale blue sky with a cream disc, a gold mosaic panel in the middle, dark and light green foliage below." Mosaic panel 3/5, foliage 3/5, gold blobs as clouds/trees 2/5. Sunflower not named. Grid 3/5.
- **B**: "a horizontally banded scene: sky with a cream disc, a dark green middle with gold flecks, a mosaic strip over greenery." Bay/sea/sunset not named. Grid 4/5.
- **C**: "a patchwork of rectangular blocks, each with a different texture; reads as a quilt." Subject "hard to name". Grid 5/5.
- **Comparison**: "variations of one picture" because of shared palette and motifs; layouts differ (central, banded, fragmented). Distinctiveness 2/5.
- **Squinting**: A clearer (gold-ringed square), B partly, C no.

## Conclusions

1. Passing the class-level carry-over gates (9–14% same-position) did not produce perceived distinctiveness. The evaluator keys on palette and motif vocabulary, which is shared by construction. Perceived difference must come from bold, nameable macro-silhouettes (as in experiment 004, scored 4/5), not from class diversity.
2. At 8x10, a radial sunflower, a bay and a pond cannot be drawn with enough resolution; the top-down pond became a patchwork.
3. Hard texture changes on piece edges again make the grid visible (3–5/5). The "shared speckle binder" does not hide boundaries.
4. Keep from B: context-dependent class readings and the role-map authoring idea. Drop: the assumption that a 14-class terrazzo vocabulary at 80 pieces can carry recognizable subjects.
